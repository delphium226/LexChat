Severity

P1
The lawyer is given something wrong or misleading and has no way to tell: a false “not found”, an unsupported in-force claim, a silently incomplete answer, a lost reply, or a dead end with no way forward.
P2
The answer is correct but incomplete, shallow or harder to verify, or the lawyer has to work around the tool to get what they asked for.
P3
Presentation, wording no lawyer has yet seen, or our own measurement and tooling.

Severity grades the defect, not the fix, and is a first-pass judgement from the transcripts and replays. Not to be confused with plan refs such as P1.1, which are rows in the fix plan.

P0.6, which research filter the twelve re-run sessions used, is now settled from the transcripts rather than assumed. The session export does not record it for those twelve, and our replays had filled the gap with “Legislation only”. We read all 50 of their questions. On 16 of them the lawyer had case law switched on, so every replay of those 16 ran without the case-law search the lawyer actually had. Replays now use what each lawyer had. The 8 questions the transcripts cannot settle are marked unknown, not guessed. Nothing a lawyer sees changes: this corrects our measurements.

Two things it found. First, the fix planned next (P4.6, wording that says “the database does not contain” something) had taken its test sessions from among those 16. The research step those lawyers actually used never contains that wording, so the test would pass whatever the fix did, and has to be chosen again before the fix is built. Second, until 13 August the research filter was not shown on screen in Conversational mode, though it was in force and could be changed. All twelve sessions ran that way.

One row opened. P0.7: the server logged which filter and mode every question ran under. One read-only query of that log would replace our reading of the transcripts with the recorded values. It can be run when the two releases are deployed.

Earlier, P3.13, a chat reply dropping part of a provision, was fixed. The lawyer who asked which provision exempts legal advice from disclosure was told about section 36(1) of the Freedom of Information (Scotland) Act 2002 and not section 36(2). The research step had found and described both, and the chat reply cut 36(2) out. The reply now keeps it. Where the research step explained a related subsection and the reply leaves it out, the research step's own sentence about it is added under that citation, word for word, as “Also in s.36: …”. That is at most three sentences, and they are never written fresh. Replayed three times, the question now gets s.36(2) in all three first answers, where it got it in one of three before. The chat reply still leaves it out on its own every time, so it is this step that delivers it. Replies carry more links, not fewer.

One thing tried and taken back out. We first added a sentence to the chat instructions telling it to keep such subsections. That also changed how it phrased the research request, and in one replay the research step went to the wrong Act (the Economic Crime and Corporate Transparency Act 2023, which uses the phrase “disclosure request”) and never looked at Freedom of Information. The instruction is gone, so the chat instructions are as they were.

Earlier, P4.1, the mode dead-end, was fixed. Replies now name the control a lawyer actually has to use: the Filters button, then Research type, then Legislation & case law. Before, four prompts between them pointed to two different controls, one of which does not exist. The system also notices when a setting changed since the previous reply, and a suggestion button can no longer offer to change a setting it cannot change. In a scripted test (refuse, change the setting, ask again), 12 of 12 answers after the change came from case law. Over the four affected sessions, replies telling the lawyer to switch or start again went from 14 to 0, and replies naming a non-existent control went from 15 to 0.

One correction to how this problem was understood. The system was thought to repeat its own earlier refusal after the setting changed. Replays show it did not: before the fix, 11 of 12 answers after a real change already searched case law. The failure in the worst-rated session was the wording. The lawyer was told to change the "research mode", so she changed the chat mode, which does not add case law. That session was also run in Deep Research, not Conversational mode as the replays had assumed.
