**Q:** What are the protected characteristics under the Equality Act 2010?
**Draft answer:** The nine protected characteristics are: age; disability; gender reassignment; marriage and civil partnership; pregnancy and maternity; race; religion or belief; sex; and sexual orientation.

## L02 — lookup — Confidence: High
**Q:** How is "employee" defined in the Employment Rights Act 1996?
**Draft answer:** An individual who has entered into or works under (or, where the employment has ceased, worked under) a contract of employment; "contract of employment" means a contract of service or apprenticeship, whether express or implied, oral or in writing.
**Required citation(s):** Employment Rights Act 1996, s.230(1)–(2).

## L05 — lookup — Confidence: High
**Q:** What is the general entitlement to information under the Freedom of Information (Scotland) Act 2002, and how quickly must a Scottish public authority respond?
**Draft answer:** A person who requests information from a Scottish public authority which holds it is entitled to be given it (s.1(1)), subject to exemptions. The authority must comply promptly and in any event not later than the twentieth working day after receipt (s.10(1)).
**Required citation(s):** Freedom of Information (Scotland) Act 2002, ss.1 and 10.

## L06 — lookup — Confidence: High
**Q:** What general principles govern interventions under the Adults with Incapacity (Scotland) Act 2000?
**Draft answer:** An intervention must benefit the adult and be the least restrictive option; account must be taken of the adult's present and past wishes, the views of the nearest relative/primary carer/guardian/attorney and others with an interest; and the adult must be encouraged to exercise residual skills and develop new ones.
**Required citation(s):** Adults with Incapacity (Scotland) Act 2000, s.1.

## L07 — lookup — Confidence: High
**Q:** At what age does a person acquire full legal capacity to enter into transactions in Scots law?

## L08 — lookup — Confidence: High

## L09 — lookup — Confidence: High
**Q:** What is the prescriptive period for most contractual obligations in Scots law?
**Draft answer:** Five years — obligations to which s.6 applies are extinguished if they have subsisted for a continuous period of five years without relevant claim or relevant acknowledgment (the "short negative prescription").
**Required citation(s):** Prescription and Limitation (Scotland) Act 1973, s.6 and Sch.1.

## L10 — lookup — Confidence: High
**Q:** What does the Consumer Rights Act 2015 require regarding the quality of goods supplied under a consumer contract?
**Draft answer:** Every contract to supply goods is treated as including a term that the quality of the goods is satisfactory — judged by the standard a reasonable person would consider satisfactory, taking account of description, price and other relevant circumstances (including fitness for purpose, appearance, freedom from minor defects, safety and durability).
**Required citation(s):** Consumer Rights Act 2015, s.9.

## L22 — jurisdiction — Confidence: High
**Q:** Is the Sewel convention recognised in statute?
**Draft answer:** Yes — s.28(8) of the Scotland Act 1998 (inserted by the Scotland Act 2016, s.2) recognises that the UK Parliament "will not normally legislate with regard to devolved matters without the consent of the Scottish Parliament". ## L31 — synthesis — Confidence: High
**Q:** What are the sanctions for owning an XL Bully dangerous dog in Scotland?
**Draft answer:** Owning an XL Bully in Scotland without a valid certificate of exemption has been a criminal offence since 31 July 2024. The XL Bully was designated under s.1 of the Dangerous Dogs Act 1991 by the Dangerous Dogs (Designated Types) (Scotland) Order 2024 (SSI 2024/31, arts 2–3). Unlawful possession (s.1(3)) or breach of the s.1(2) prohibitions is punishable on summary conviction by up to six months' imprisonment, a fine not exceeding level 5 on the standard scale, or both (s.1(7)); on conviction the court must order destruction of the dog unless satisfied it does not constitute a danger to public safety, and may disqualify the offender from having custody of a dog (s.4). Exemption conditions (muzzle and lead in public, secure keeping, third-party insurance, microchipping, neutering) are set by the Dangerous Dogs (Compensation and Exemption Schemes) (Scotland) Order 2024 (SSI 2024/70, arts 5–9); breaching a condition exposes the owner to the s.1(3) sanctions. A strong answer notes *R (Coulthard) v SSEFRA* ([2024] EWHC 3252 (Admin); appeal dismissed [2025] EWCA Civ 1671), upholding the equivalent E&W designation as persuasive authority.
**Required citation(s):** Dangerous Dogs Act 1991, ss.1 and 4; SSI 2024/31; SSI 2024/70.
